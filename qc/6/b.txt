# QC6 Quantum–HPC Integration

This skill explores how quantum computing can be integrated into high-performance computing (HPC) environments and workflows.
Learners examine hybrid quantum–classical approaches in which classical HPC resources prepare circuits, execute them on simulators or quantum processors, and post-process measurement results.
The skill focuses on integration patterns and constraints that matter in HPC settings, including co-locating quantum workloads with classical pre-/post-processing, managing latency and throughput, and assessing end-to-end time-to-solution.
Representative examples (e.g., chemistry/materials, optimization, and machine learning) are used to illustrate both opportunities and practical limitations such as noise, limited throughput, and the impact of sampling on runtime.

## Learning Outcomes

* Describe the role of quantum processors as accelerators in hybrid quantum–classical workflows in HPC settings.
* Differentiate between classical simulation of quantum circuits and execution on quantum hardware, and explain why each is used in HPC workflows.
* Describe, at a practical level, how hybrid workflows iterate between circuit execution, sampling, and classical post-processing (including parameter updates when relevant).
* Analyze end-to-end performance considerations for quantum–HPC workflows (latency, throughput, sampling cost, and repetition due to noise) and how they affect time-to-solution.
* Discuss representative HPC-relevant application areas where quantum workflows are being explored (e.g., chemistry/materials, optimization, and machine learning) and what “success” would look like compared to classical baselines.
